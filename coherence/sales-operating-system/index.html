Sales Operating System

Stage progression should reflect a customer decision, not a seller feeling. I built this system around the evidence that an enterprise deal is actually moving: executive priority, consequence of delay, access, commercial champion behavior, decision path, and a customer-owned next step.

The operating principle

Evidence beats optimism.

MEDDPICC is useful when it changes seller behavior and inspection. It is less useful as a checklist completed late in the deal. This design treats qualification as an evolving evidence record tied to stage gates and buyer behavior.

Product champion ≠ commercial champion

Product championBelieves in the solution, understands the use case, gives product or process insight, and may advocate. Valuable ally. Not a MEDDPICC Champion by enthusiasm alone.
Commercial championHas organizational influence, sells internally, understands the business and funding path, creates access to power and the economic buyer, coaches the seller, and takes customer-owned action.
SignalStrong evidenceWeak evidence
Commercial championCreates access, explains internal politics, tests the message, knows the funding path, and moves work when the seller is absent.Likes the product, takes meetings, and helps with requirements but keeps access contained.
Economic buyerHas confirmed the problem matters enough to fund and has engaged on value, risk, or timing.A name in CRM with no direct interaction.
Compelling eventA dated business consequence exists and the customer is acting against it.A seller-created close date or vague urgency.
Multi-threadingUsers, executives, technical stakeholders, procurement, and skeptics are represented where relevant.Several contacts from the same team who all depend on one sponsor.
Decision processCustomer has named the people, criteria, sequence, and commercial path.“Legal next” with no owner, date, or approval logic.

Stage governance

Each stage answers a different customer question.

Qualified

Customer question: is this problem worth examining?

Gate: material problem, plausible fit, and a real reason to spend discovery time.

Discovery

Customer question: what is happening now and what does it cost us?

Gate: current state, impact, priority, stakeholders, and the beginning of measurable value.

Solution alignment

Customer question: does this approach solve the right problem?

Gate: buying group aligns on problem, proof, success measures, and required participation.

Business decision

Customer question: should we invest?

Gate: economic buyer and buying group evaluate value, risk, scope, funding, and timing.

Contracting

Customer question: can we execute the decision?

Gate: commercial, security, legal, procurement, and implementation dependencies have named owners and dates.

Closed

Customer question: what happens now?

Gate: delivery launches, or loss/recycle reason is recorded with learning intact.

Example artifact

A champion test should make a deal less comfortable, not more comfortable.

Instead of asking “Do we have a champion?”, the seller tests observable behavior and whether the relationship has moved from product advocacy to commercial influence.

Commercial champion test

Access testWho will you bring into the next conversation, and why do they matter?
Message testHow would you explain the business case to your executive sponsor without us in the room?
Consequence testWhat happens internally if this slips a quarter?
Action testWhat will you do before our next meeting that moves the decision forward?
