Coherence · public case-study preview

Sell the operating change, not the AI activity.

A designed enterprise GTM system for turning AI pressure into an evidence-based decision, a governable implementation, and measurable business capability.

Designed, not disguised.

Coherence is a concept AI-transformation firm used to demonstrate offer architecture, enterprise selling, RevOps governance, delivery, and expansion design. This preview does not claim market-validated services or production deployments.

The design challenge

Companies are deploying AI faster than their operating systems can absorb it.

Boards expect strategy and leverage. Teams inherit scattered experiments, weak data, unclear ownership, brittle controls, and uneven adoption. Coherence starts with the work, the business pressure, and what the organization can realistically sustain.

Commercial architecture

Three motions. One expansion logic.

Individual learning, team transformation, and enterprise transformation require different buyers and operating rhythms. The design connects them without forcing every customer into the same sales process.

Individual

Build shared language

An assessment and foundations path helps practitioners understand the work and surface potential product champions who may become commercial champions.

Team

Prove the first change

A functional team diagnoses work, assesses readiness, and establishes the first controlled implementation.

Enterprise

Install an operating model

Executives align work, architecture, governance, adoption, and value realization through a phased roadmap.

Revenue architecture

Customer decisions govern the stages.

Diagnosis and business-case development are required work, not extra administrative stages. Forecast category remains separate from stage.

Qualified

The customer agrees a material problem is worth examining.

Discovery

The buying group shares current state, impact, priority, and context.

Solution alignment

Stakeholders align on the problem, proof, success, and path.

Business decision

Executives evaluate value, risk, scope, investment, and timing.

Contracting

The parties complete commercial, security, legal, and procurement work.

Closed

Delivery launches or the decision and learning are recorded.

Evidence over optimism

Product champions create interest. Commercial champions create movement.

MEDDPICC already expects a Champion to have influence, sell internally, and take the seller toward power. Coherence names the distinction because teams too often promote the person who likes the product into a role they have not earned.

Product champion

  • Believes in the solution and understands the use case
  • Provides product, process, and user insight
  • May advocate with peers or help shape requirements
  • Can be an important ally without organizational influence
  • Does not satisfy MEDDPICC Champion through enthusiasm alone

Commercial champion

  • Has organizational influence and credibility
  • Sells the initiative internally when the seller is absent
  • Understands the business, funding, and decision path
  • Creates access to power and the economic buyer
  • Takes customer-owned action that moves the decision forward
Deal inspection

Do we have a commercial champion, or someone who really likes the product?

A credible commercial champion requires observed evidence of influence, access, and internal action. A high engagement score cannot compensate for missing power or behavior. Product champion → commercial champion → power is a progression to test, not an assumption.

Example application

A focused proof before enterprise-wide implementation

A software company has multiple GTM teams experimenting with AI. The CRO expects productivity, the CIO is concerned about data, and RevOps is absorbing manual exception handling. The recommended first move is a focused assessment of seller preparation, account research, CRM writeback, and exceptions, not a sweeping implementation.

The work proceeds only if the CRO and CIO agree on measures, data boundaries, business ownership, and required customer participation.
